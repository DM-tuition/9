Why students lose marks — and how we fix it

Chemistry punishes two things above all: the quantitative work — moles, concentration, percentage yield — where one unit slip wipes out every mark on the question, and the extended-response answers where students describe a process when the examiner asked them to explain it.

I scored a grade 9 in GCSE Chemistry, and I sat the paper recently enough to remember exactly which steps earn the marks. Moles calculations become a method you repeat rather than a puzzle you dread, and the required practicals turn into reliable marks.

  • Bonding, structure & properties
  • Quantitative chemistry (moles, yield, concentration)
  • Chemical changes & electrolysis
  • Energy changes & rates of reaction
